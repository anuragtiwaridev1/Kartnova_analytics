# Analytics Catalogue — what was built, why, and how it is useful

Every analysis answers a business question and ends in a decision. **Py** = Python, **SQL** = `sql/`, **BI** = Power BI/DAX.
The same business rules (net revenue, refunds, COGS reversal) are implemented identically in all three layers and reconciled.

| # | Analysis | Where | Why it was built | How it is useful (decision it supports) | Key techniques |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Data-quality audit & cleaning** | Py `01`, SQL `05` | Every number downstream is only as good as the data. Defects are quantified, fixed by explicit rules and logged (`data_quality_log.csv`); SQL assertions re-test integrity | Trust: reviewers see 289 duplicate orders, 450 messy city names, 25 impossible delivery dates, 8 impossible ages etc. were found and how each was handled | Profiling, FK/orphan checks, imputation by group median, business-rule assertions |
| 2 | **Executive KPI scorecard** | Py `02`, SQL q01, BI p1 | One agreed definition of revenue, margin, AOV, returns, cancellations | Board/leadership view of health; growth 129% → 50% YoY at a stable ~32% margin | Aggregation, YoY, net-vs-kept revenue |
| 3 | **Monthly trend, MoM/YoY, YTD, rolling avg** | Py `02`, SQL q02–q03, BI time-intel | Growth rates tell direction; YoY removes seasonality; rolling average removes noise | Run-rate tracking vs last year; spotting momentum change early | `LAG`, windowed `SUM`, `TOTALYTD`, `DATESINPERIOD` |
| 4 | **Seasonality index** | Py `02`, BI | Festive (Oct–Nov) demand drives inventory and campaign timing | Plan stock, staffing and ad budget ahead of the Diwali peak | Ratio-to-average index |
| 5 | **Category profitability & margin-vs-returns portfolio** | Py `02`, SQL q04, BI p2 | Revenue ≠ profit; returns silently erode margin | Electronics = 58% of revenue but 22% margin; Fashion earns 55% margin but 19% returns → different playbooks per category | Margin after returns, bubble matrix |
| 6 | **Pareto / ABC product analysis** | Py `02`, SQL q05, BI p2 | 80/20 rule: focus where money is made | A-class SKUs never out of stock; C-tail reviewed for delisting; 25% of SKUs = 80% revenue | Cumulative share windows, `RANKX` |
| 7 | **Regional & delivery performance** | Py `02`, SQL q07/q15, BI p5 | Logistics cost/experience differs by geography | North-East avg 7.7 days, 54% late → carrier / warehouse decision | `RANK` within group, SLA metrics |
| 8 | **RFM segmentation + K-Means** | Py `03`, SQL q09, BI p3 | Customers are not equal; behaviour-based targeting beats blanket promotions | Champions = 17% of customers / 43% of revenue → loyalty; "Can't Lose Them"/"At Risk" → win-back; "Lost" → stop discounting | Quintile scoring, silhouette-selected k, `NTILE`, calculated columns |
| 9 | **Cohort retention** | Py `04`, SQL q10, BI p3 | Aggregate growth hides a leaky bucket; cohorts reveal true retention | M1/M3/M6 retention 18.8% / 16.3% / 13.8%; Referral & Organic retain best → acquisition mix | Cohort matrix, right-censoring, size-weighted curves |
| 10 | **Repeat-purchase funnel & inter-order gap** | SQL q11 | When do customers return and how many do? | 62% place a 2nd order after ~95 days → time the post-purchase journey around day 60–90 | `LAG`, `ROW_NUMBER` |
| 11 | **Revenue concentration** | SQL q12, BI | Dependency risk on few customers | Top 10% of customers = 49% of revenue → VIP retention is a board-level priority | `NTILE`, cumulative share |
| 12 | **Churn prediction model** | Py `05` | Retention spend should go where it changes behaviour | Ranked list of customers by churn probability × value at risk (ROC-AUC 0.83); top-4 deciles capture 54% of churners; ₹5.8 Cr at "High" risk | Leakage-safe temporal split, logistic vs gradient boosting, permutation importance, decile lift |
| 13 | **CAC, 12-month LTV, LTV:CAC, payback** | Py `06`, SQL q14, BI p4 | "Cheapest channel" is the wrong question; profit per rupee is the right one | Referral/Organic 11–12× with 1-month payback; Paid Search 2.5× (<3× benchmark) → re-allocate budget | Spend ÷ acquisitions, mature-cohort LTV |
| 14 | **Market-basket (association rules)** | Py `07`, SQL q18 | What sells together drives bundles and recommendations | Laptops → Accessories (lift 13×, conf 47%); Smartphones ↔ Headphones (66% conf.) → bundle offers & cart cross-sell | Support/confidence/lift from scratch, self-join |
| 15 | **Hypothesis tests & discount effectiveness** | Py `08` | "Looks different" isn't evidence; policy changes need significance *and* effect size | COD cancels 8.9% vs 3.3% (p<0.001) → nudge to prepaid; discount depth has **no** relationship with units (ρ=0.004) but cuts margin and, >20%, lifts returns to 12.9% | χ², Kruskal-Wallis, Mann-Whitney, bootstrap CI, Spearman |
| 16 | **Discount-cap what-if** | Py `08`, BI p6 | Convert a finding into a decision tool | Capping discounts at 10% ≈ +₹83.9 lakh (+10.9% of gross profit), if volume holds | Scenario analysis, `SUMX`, what-if parameter |
| 17 | **Revenue forecast with backtest** | Py `09` | Finance/supply chain need a defensible 6-month outlook | Jan–Jun 2026 forecast with 80% interval; model chosen on rolling-origin out-of-sample MAPE (14.5%), not in-sample fit | Log-trend + seasonal regression, rolling-origin CV |
| 18 | **Returns root-cause** | SQL q17, BI p5 | Fix the cause, not the symptom | Fashion returns led by Size/Fit → sizing guide; Electronics → QC | `RANK` within group, share-of-total windows |
| 19 | **Reconciliation across tools** | `run_sql_pipeline.py`, DAX Q1 | An analyst who can't tie numbers across tools isn't trusted | Same totals in Python, SQL and Power BI | Cross-validation |
